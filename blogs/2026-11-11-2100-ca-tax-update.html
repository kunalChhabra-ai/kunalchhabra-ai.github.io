Executive Summary: Under Rule 37A of the CGST Rules, registered taxpayers must reverse Input Tax Credit (ITC) if their suppliers fail to file GSTR-3B by 30th September following the financial year. Missing this statutory deadline triggers mandatory interest liabilities under Section 50 and potential recovery proceedings by the CBIC.

Recent advisories and circulars issued by the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) have dramatically increased scrutiny on Input Tax Credit (ITC) claims. For Indian startups, CFOs, and enterprise tax teams, managing supplier compliance is no longer optional—it is a critical driver of working capital health. Rule 37A of the CGST Rules introduces strict timelines for ITC reversal when a supplier fails to deposit collected GST via their GSTR-3B return.

Understanding Rule 37A Mechanics and Statutory Timelines

Rule 37A comes into play when a recipient claims ITC on an invoice in their GSTR-2B, but the corresponding supplier fails to file their GSTR-3B return for that specific tax period. If the anomaly persists, the recipient is statutorily mandated to reverse the claimed ITC on or before 30th September of the immediately succeeding financial year.

Key regulatory aspects to keep in mind include:

  • Interest Liability: If the reversal is not completed by the due date, interest under Section 50 of the CGST Act at 18% per annum applies retrospectively from the date the credit was utilized.
  • Re-availability of Credit: The good news for businesses is that once the defaulting supplier eventually files their GSTR-3B and pays the tax, the reversed ITC can be re-claimed by the recipient without any upper time limit.
  • System Integration: The GST Portal now auto-populates many of these variances, flagging discrepancies between GSTR-2B and GSTR-3B during routine departmental audits.
