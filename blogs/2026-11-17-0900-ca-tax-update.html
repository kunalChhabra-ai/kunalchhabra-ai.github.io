Executive Summary: Receiving Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) requires strict adherence to Reserve Bank of India (RBI) guidelines under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA). Founders must file Form FC-GPR via the FIRMS portal within 30 days of share allotment to avoid severe Late Submission Fees (LSF) or compounding proceedings.

Securing cross-border capital marks a monumental milestone for Indian tech ventures and private limited companies. However, capital infusion from non-resident entities triggers immediate mandatory regulatory reporting via the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) FIRMS (Foreign Investment Reporting and Management System) portal. Failing to comply with Foreign Exchange Management (Mode of Acquisition and Transfer of Permissible Non-Debt Instruments) Regulations can halt subsequent rounds of funding and attract steep penalties.

Understanding the Mechanics of Form FC-GPR

When an Indian unlisted company issues equity shares, compulsorily convertible debentures (CCDs), or preference shares to a foreign investor against inward remittance, it must report the transaction through Form FC-GPR (Foreign Currency-Gross Provisional Return). As per RBI Master Directions, the statutory timeline for filing this form is strictly 30 days from the date of capital instrument allotment.

Before initiating the FC-GPR filing on the single master form (SMF) module, corporate finance teams must fulfill foundational prerequisites:

  • Obtain an Entity User ID on the FIRMS portal administered by the RBI.
  • Register the Business User (BU) profile authenticated by the authorized signatory.
  • File the Advance Reporting Form (ARF) to report the inward remittance received from the foreign investor within the prescribed window.
  • Secure a Know Your Customer (KYC) report for the foreign investor from the remitting bank abroad.

Step-by-Step Actionable Compliance Roadmap for Founders

Executing an airtight FDI compliance framework demands systematic coordination between legal counsels, authorized dealer (AD) banks, and chartered accountants. Implement this four-step roadmap upon receiving foreign funds:

  • Step 1: Track Inward Remittance & FIRC Verification: Ensure the foreign investor remits funds through banking channels directly into the company’s designated bank account. Download the Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) and Know Your Customer (KYC) report issued by the AD bank immediately.
  • Step 2: Valuation Certification by CA/Merchant Banker: Issue equity shares or convertible instruments at a price not less than the valuation computed as per internationally accepted pricing methodologies certified by a Chartered Accountant or SEBI-registered Merchant Banker in compliance with FEMA guidelines.
  • Step 3: Board Meeting & Share Allotment: Convene a Board of Directors meeting within 60 days of receiving the remittance to formally allot shares. Update the MCA portal via Form PAS-3 simultaneously alongside FEMA filings.
  • Step 4: FIRMS Portal Submission & AD Bank Clearance: Upload Form FC-GPR with mandatory attachments—including the CS Certificate, Valuation Report, FIRC, and KYC—on the RBI FIRMS portal. Address query loops raised by your designated AD bank promptly to secure the finalized Auto-Acknowledgement.
